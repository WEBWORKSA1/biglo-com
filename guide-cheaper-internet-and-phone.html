Cheaper Internet & Phone Plans: How to Cut Your Connectivity Bill

Figure out the speed and data you really need, compare carriers and ISPs, and negotiate promotional rates that stick.

By the Biglo Editorial Team·Updated 2026-09-20·6 min read

Key takeaways
  • Most households need far less speed than premium tiers sell.
  • Budget carriers often run on the same major networks.
  • Buying your own modem/router can eliminate monthly rental fees.
  • Promo rates expire — calendar the end date and renegotiate.

1. Know the speed you need

Streaming HD video uses roughly 5–8 Mbps per stream and 4K about 15–25 Mbps. Add up your household's simultaneous use and add headroom — that's your target tier, not the fastest plan on offer.

2. Own your equipment

Equipment rental fees are recurring. A compatible modem/router often pays for itself within a year. Confirm compatibility with your ISP first.

3. Check budget mobile carriers

Many lower-cost carriers (MVNOs) use the big networks' towers. If coverage in your area is the same, the price difference is pure savings. Check your actual monthly data usage in your phone settings before choosing.

4. Negotiate when promos end

Set a reminder a month before a promotional rate ends. Call retention with a competing offer; ISPs often extend promos rather than lose a customer.

5. Look for assistance programs

Many providers offer low-income or senior plans. Ask directly — eligibility can be broader than people expect.

FAQ

Is 1 Gbps worth it?

Only for heavy simultaneous use — large households, big file uploads, multiple 4K streams plus gaming. Most homes are fine on much less.

Can I keep my number when switching carriers?

Yes, number porting is standard. Don't cancel your old service until the port completes.

Are prepaid plans worse?

Not necessarily. Many prepaid plans run on the same networks; the main differences are priority during congestion and perks.
