Is Solar Worth It? A No-Hype Guide to Payback and Savings

How to estimate solar payback, what drives cost, incentives to check, and when solar doesn't make sense.

By the Biglo Editorial Team·Updated 2026-09-20·8 min read

Editorial independence: our guides are written without input from partners. Some links may earn us a commission. How we make money.

Key takeaways
  • Payback depends mostly on your electricity rate, sunlight and system cost.
  • Check current incentives and net-metering rules where you live.
  • Get multiple installer quotes — pricing varies widely.
  • Shaded roofs and planned moves weaken the case.

1. The simple payback formula

Payback years ≈ (system cost − incentives) ÷ annual bill savings. Plug your numbers into the Solar Savings calculator for a quick estimate.

2. What drives the numbers

Local electricity rates, sun hours, roof orientation and shade, system size, and how your utility credits exported power (net metering) matter more than panel brand.

3. Incentives and rules

Tax credits, rebates and net-metering policies change. Verify what's currently available for your address before signing.

4. Buy, loan or lease?

Cash purchase usually has the best lifetime return; loans spread cost; leases/PPAs need no upfront money but capture less of the savings. Compare total 20–25 year cost, not the monthly payment.

5. Get multiple quotes

Installer pricing varies significantly. Compare at least three quotes on cost per watt, equipment, warranties and production estimates. Get matched with local installers.

6. When solar isn't worth it

Heavy shade, a roof that needs replacing soon, very low electricity rates or planning to move soon can all push payback beyond what's attractive.

FAQ

How long do solar panels last?

Panels commonly carry 25-year performance warranties; inverters often need replacement sooner.

Do panels work in cloudy climates?

Yes, at reduced output. Your local production estimate is what matters.

Can I add batteries later?

Usually yes. Ask the installer to design the system to be battery-ready.
