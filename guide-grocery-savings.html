Grocery Savings: Cut Your Food Bill Without Eating Worse

Unit pricing, meal planning, store brands, cashback apps and timing — the habits that shrink grocery spend week after week.

By the Biglo Editorial Team·Updated 2026-09-20·6 min read

Editorial independence: our guides are written without input from partners. Some links may earn us a commission. How we make money.

Key takeaways
  • Compare unit prices, not package prices.
  • Plan meals around what's on sale, not the other way around.
  • Store brands are often made to similar standards at lower cost.
  • Stack store loyalty offers with cashback apps.

1. Always check unit price

Bigger isn't always cheaper. Use the shelf-tag unit price or the Unit Price Compare tool.

2. Plan meals from the sales flyer

Build the week's menu around discounted proteins and produce. Cook once, eat twice — leftovers are the cheapest lunch.

3. Switch to store brands

Try the store brand for staples — flour, rice, canned goods, spices, cleaning supplies. Keep the name brand only where you can taste the difference.

4. Stack offers

Loyalty-card digital coupons, cashback apps and card rewards can often be combined on the same trip.

5. Cut waste

Food you throw away is money you threw away. Shop your fridge first, freeze extras, and keep a running list.

6. Watch Biglo deals

Grocery and meal-kit promos are posted daily on Biglo Deals.

FAQ

Is buying in bulk worth it?

For non-perishables you'll use before they expire, often yes — check the unit price.

Are meal kits cheaper than groceries?

Usually not at full price, but introductory offers can be good value and reduce waste.

How much should I budget for groceries?

It varies by household size and region. Track two months of spending to set a realistic baseline.
