Car Insurance: 10 Proven Ways to Pay Less Without Losing Coverage

What actually moves your premium, how to compare quotes like-for-like, and the discounts most drivers never ask for.

By the Biglo Editorial Team·Updated 2026-09-20·7 min read

Key takeaways
  • Compare at least three quotes with identical limits and deductibles.
  • Ask for every discount by name — they are rarely applied automatically.
  • Review coverage on older cars; collision may cost more than it protects.
  • Shop 3–4 weeks before renewal for the best rates.

1. Compare like-for-like

Price differences between insurers for the same driver can be large. The key is comparing identical liability limits, deductibles and add-ons — otherwise a “cheap” quote may simply be less coverage.

2. Ask for discounts by name

Common ones: multi-policy (bundle), multi-car, good driver, good student, defensive-driving course, anti-theft device, paperless, paid-in-full, and low mileage. Ask the agent to list every discount you qualify for.

3. Adjust deductibles

Moving from a low to a higher deductible lowers your premium. Match the deductible to what your emergency fund can cover.

4. Reconsider coverage on older cars

If your car's value is low, compare the yearly cost of collision and comprehensive with the car's value minus the deductible. When the premium approaches that number, it may be time to drop it.

5. Try usage-based insurance

Telematics programs track driving habits through an app or device. Safe, low-mileage drivers often earn meaningful discounts.

6. Keep your record and credit healthy

In many regions, claims history, violations and (where permitted) credit-based insurance scores affect pricing. Improving these lowers future premiums.

7. Pay in full and go paperless

Installment fees add up. Paying the 6- or 12-month premium upfront typically costs less overall.

8. Re-shop every renewal

Loyalty isn't always rewarded. Get fresh quotes each renewal — Biglo can match you in about 2 minutes.

FAQ

How often should I compare car insurance?

At least once a year, and any time you move, buy a car, add a driver or your record changes.

Does bundling home and auto always save money?

Often, but not always. Compare the bundle total against the best separate policies.

Will a claim raise my rates?

At-fault claims usually do. For small damage, compare the repair cost to your deductible and potential rate increase.
