How to Lower Your Monthly Bills: The 12-Step Playbook

A practical, step-by-step system to cut insurance, internet, phone, energy and subscription costs — with scripts you can use today.

By the Biglo Editorial Team·Updated 2026-09-20·9 min read

Editorial independence: our guides are written without input from partners. Some links may earn us a commission. How we make money.

Key takeaways
  • Start with the three biggest recurring bills — they hold most of the savings.
  • Competing quotes are your leverage: providers price-match to keep you.
  • Put a calendar reminder 30 days before every renewal date.
  • Audit subscriptions quarterly; most households pay for at least one they don't use.

1. List every recurring bill

Pull the last 60 days of bank and card statements and list every charge that repeats: insurance, internet, mobile, streaming, gym, software, utilities, loan payments. Write the amount, renewal date and whether it's under contract. You can't cut what you can't see — and most people underestimate their recurring spend.

2. Rank by size and flexibility

Sort the list by annual cost. Insurance, housing-related utilities and phone/internet usually top it. Then flag which are negotiable today (no contract, or renewal within 60 days). That shortlist is where you start.

3. Get 2–3 competing quotes

Before you call any provider, know the market price. Use a comparison service or Biglo's free quote matching to get real numbers for the same coverage or speed. A competing offer turns a polite request into a negotiation.

4. Call retention, not sales

Ask for the retention or loyalty department. Script: “I've been a customer for X years. I have an offer for $Y for the same service. I'd rather stay — can you match or beat it?” Be quiet after you ask. If the first rep can't help, thank them, hang up and try again later.

5. Right-size what you actually use

Check real usage: data per line, internet speed needed for your household, coverage limits and deductibles. Many people overpay for unlimited data they don't use or internet tiers far above what streaming requires.

6. Bundle — but verify

Home + auto bundles and family phone plans often discount 10–25%, but always compare the bundle total against the best separate offers. A bundle is only a deal if it wins on the total.

7. Raise deductibles wisely

If you have an emergency fund, raising an insurance deductible can lower premiums noticeably. Only do it if you could comfortably pay the higher deductible tomorrow.

8. Kill zombie subscriptions

Cancel anything you haven't used in 30 days. Rotate streaming services instead of holding them all — subscribe for the show, binge, cancel. Use the Subscription Audit tool to see the annual total.

9. Cut energy use at the source

Smart thermostats, LED bulbs, weather-stripping and running big appliances off-peak all compound. Where available, compare energy suppliers or look at solar with the Solar Savings calculator.

10. Automate and set reminders

Autopay discounts are free money. Add renewal reminders 30 days early so you shop before auto-renewal locks you in for another year.

11. Refinance high-interest debt

Interest is a bill too. If your credit has improved, a lower-rate loan or balance transfer can cut the monthly payment and total interest. Run the numbers in the Loan calculator first.

12. Repeat every 6–12 months

Prices creep. Put a recurring “bill day” on your calendar twice a year and repeat steps 3–4. Most savings come from doing this consistently, not once.

FAQ

How much can I realistically save?

It depends on your current bills, but combining insurance shopping, a cheaper phone plan and a subscription cleanup commonly saves hundreds per year. Use the Bill Savings calculator for your own estimate.

Will shopping for insurance quotes hurt my credit?

Insurance quotes typically use soft inquiries that don't affect your credit score. Loan pre-qualification also usually uses a soft pull — always confirm with the lender.

Is negotiating bills awkward?

Retention teams handle these calls all day. Being polite, specific and having a competing offer makes it routine.
